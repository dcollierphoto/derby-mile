Skate the diamond

Here's what Diamond does, going into each turn:

  1. On the straightaway, stay about 5 or 6 feet off the inside line.That gives you room to make your turn round instead of sharp.
  2. In the middle of the turn, get close to the inside line, about 2 feet off.That spot is called the apex. Getting close here keeps your lap short.
  3. Coming out, drift out a little and push.Let the turn open up and use your strides to power down the straightaway.

Why it works: Hugger takes a short path, but her turns are so tight she has to slow down and never gets back to full speed. Wide never slows down, but her laps are way longer. Diamond keeps her laps short and her speed up.

Laps or a real mile?

Counting laps and measuring distance are two different races. Here's Diamond against the Mile Line for the same 60 seconds. Watch the bars: they show how far each skater has gone.

+98 ft

In the same 60 seconds, the Mile Line goes farther, even though Diamond skates more laps.

  • Coach counting laps? Skate the Diamond. Shorter laps, so you get more of them.
  • Measuring a real mile? Skate the Mile Line: about 12 feet off the inside on the straightaways and 6 feet off in the turns, so you never have to slow down.
