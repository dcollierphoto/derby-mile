Derby Mile

How to skate a faster lap, and what your lap count says about your speed.

The race

Three skaters with the exact same legs race 5 laps. The only difference is the path they take.

HuggerHugs the inside line0.0 laps
DiamondSkates the diamond0.0 laps
WideStays out wide0.0 laps

Skating a mile? If the mile is measured by real distance, stay a few feet out the whole way, even in the turns, so you never have to slow down. Fewer, longer laps win. If your coach counts laps instead, skate the diamond.

Skate the diamond

Here's what Diamond does, going into each turn:

  1. Before the turn, stay a few feet off the inside line.That gives you room to make your turn round instead of sharp.
  2. In the middle of the turn, get close to the inside line.That spot is called the apex. Touching close here keeps your lap short.
  3. Coming out, drift out a little and push.Let the turn open up and use your strides to power down the straightaway.

Why it works: Hugger takes the shortest path, but the turns are so tight she has to slow down. Wide never slows down, but her laps are way longer. Diamond keeps her laps short and her speed up.

How it works: 5 minutes is 300 seconds. Divide 300 by your laps to get your time per lap. A good derby lap is about 155 to 170 feet long, so a mile (5,280 feet) is about 31 to 34 laps.
