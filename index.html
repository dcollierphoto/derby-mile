Where her speed comes from

We don't know her top speed, so the page works backward from something she has actually done. Derby skaters are tested on how many laps they can skate in 5 minutes (WFTDA's minimum is 27). Her best is 31 laps. The page finds the top speed that produces that many laps on the line shown, including the standing start and the slowdown.

After that, the dotted ideal line shows the fastest line for her speed, and you can drag her line to see what a different line would do with the same legs.

How it was figured

  • Track shape is the official WFTDA layout: inside turns are 12 ft 6 in radius, straightaways are 35 ft, and the track is 13 ft wide going into a turn and 15 ft coming out.
  • She can only lean so hard in a turn. I used 0.6 g of sideways force, so cornering speed grows with the square root of the turn radius. Tighter turns force her to slow down.
  • She speeds up quickly from a stop and more slowly near top speed, and scrubs speed gently, like legs rather than brakes.
  • She starts from a stop at the jammer line and never stops after that. Laps are counted at the jammer line.
  • Most laps means the shortest time per lap. Fastest mile means the highest average speed when real distance is measured, not laps; it picks the shortest line that lets her keep almost all of her speed.
  • Human wobble adds small random drift from lap to lap. The default line already cuts in early on the way into a turn and drifts wide on the way out.
  • These are a model's numbers, not measurements. Her real lap times would make it sharper.

Sources: WFTDA track layout guide, WFTDA minimum skills companion.
