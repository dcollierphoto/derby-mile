How it was figured

  • Track shape is the official WFTDA layout: inside turns are 12 ft 6 in radius around center points 17 ft 6 in from the middle, with 35 ft straightaways. The track is 13 ft wide going into a turn and 15 ft coming out.
  • The skater can only lean so hard in a turn. I used 0.45 g of sideways force (about how hard a car corners in a parking lot), which makes cornering speed grow with the square root of the turn radius.
  • She speeds up slowly (2.5 ft/s per second) and scrubs speed slowly, like real legs, not brakes.
  • The diamond line here is wide on the straightaways and touching the inside at each turn's apex, which is how WFTDA's skills guide describes it.
  • She starts from a stop at the jammer line and never stops after that. When she changes lines from one lap to the next, she drifts across the straightaway between the jammer and pivot lines, so laps flow into each other.
  • Real skaters aren't perfect, so her line wanders a little, she sometimes misses an apex by drifting wide or turning in early, and her crossover strides add a slight side-to-side in the turns. She gets steadier as she picks up speed and looser when she's slow or tired. It costs her a few seconds over the mile compared with the perfect line.
  • The mile is a simulation: top speed fades from 17.5 to about 11.5 mph, and each lap's line wobbles around the best line for her speed.
  • Her line stays realistic: no more than about 7.5 ft off the inside line on the straightaways, and about a foot of space at each turn's apex instead of scraping the tape.

Sources: WFTDA track layout guide, WFTDA minimum skills companion.
